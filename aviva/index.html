🎯 Understanding Phishing Attacks

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cybersecurity attack where criminals impersonate legitimate organizations to steal sensitive information like passwords, credit card numbers, or personal data. These attacks have become increasingly sophisticated and can cost companies millions of dollars.

📊 Phishing Statistics:
• 90% of data breaches start with phishing emails
• Average cost of a successful phishing attack: $4.65 million
• Phishing attempts increased by 65% in 2024
• 1 in 4 employees will click on a phishing link

Why Attackers Target You:

  • You have access to company systems and sensitive data
  • Your email credentials can be used to attack others
  • Financial transactions you process can be redirected
  • Customer information you handle is valuable on black markets

🚩 Advanced Red Flag Recognition

Domain and Email Analysis:

  • Look-Alike Domains: avivametalz.com, aviva-metals.com, avivarnetals.com
  • Wrong Extensions: avivametals.net, avivametals.org instead of .com
  • Subdomain Tricks: avivametals.com.secure-verify.net (real domain is secure-verify.net)
  • Homograph Attacks: Using similar-looking characters from different alphabets
✅ How to Verify Email Addresses:
1. Check the actual email domain, not just the display name
2. Look for what comes AFTER the @ symbol
3. Verify the domain matches official company communications
4. Be suspicious of generic addresses (noreply@, admin@, support@)

Social Engineering Tactics:

  • Urgency: "Act now!", "Within 24 hours", "Immediate action required"
  • Authority: Impersonating executives, IT staff, or government agencies
  • Fear: Threats of account closure, legal action, or job loss
  • Greed: Promises of money, prizes, or exclusive opportunities
  • Curiosity: "You won't believe this!", mysterious attachments
  • Trust: Impersonating colleagues, partners, or customers

Attachment Red Flags:

  • Dangerous Extensions: .exe, .bat, .scr, .vbs, .js, .cmd
  • Double Extensions: document.pdf.exe, invoice.doc.js
  • Macro-Enabled Files: .docm, .xlsm (can contain malicious code)
  • Archive Files: .zip, .rar, .7z containing executables

🔍 Real-World Example #1: Look-Alike Domain Attack

From: security@avivametalsinc.com
To: you@avivametals.com
Subject: Security Alert - Verify Your Account Now

Your account has been flagged for unusual login activity from an unknown location.
Please verify your identity immediately to prevent account suspension.

Click here to verify: https://avivametalsinc.com/verify?id=8472

This link expires in 2 hours.

IT Security Team
Aviva Metals Inc.

🚨 What's Wrong:

  • Domain is "avivametalsinc.com" - notice the extra "inc" before .com
  • Real domain is "avivametals.com" - subtle one-word difference!
  • Creates urgency with "2 hours" expiration
  • Threatens account suspension to cause panic
  • URL parameter (id=8472) looks technical to seem legitimate

✅ Always check: The exact domain spelling AFTER the @ symbol. One letter difference = phishing!

🔍 Real-World Example #2: CEO Fraud (Business Email Compromise)

From: Norman Lazarus <nlazarus.urgent@gmail.com>
To: you@avivametals.com

Please process an urgent wire transfer:
Amount: $45,000
Account: 7834-2918-4456
Bank: Wells Fargo

This deal closes in 30 minutes. Send confirmation when complete.
DO NOT discuss this with anyone - client confidentiality.

Norman Lazarus
President, Aviva Metals Inc.

🚨 Red Flags:

  • Gmail address - Company president uses corporate email (@avivametals.com)
  • Extreme urgency - "30 minutes" creates panic
  • Bypasses approval - Normal wire transfers require proper workflow
  • Demands secrecy - "DO NOT discuss" prevents verification
  • Unusual channel - Large transactions not done via email

✅ What to do: Call Norman using the company directory number. NEVER process urgent wire transfers based solely on email!

🔍 Advanced Verification Techniques

The "Hover Test":

Before clicking any link, hover your mouse over it to see the actual URL. If it doesn't match what the text says, it's suspicious!

Example: Link text says "portal.avivametals.com" but hovering shows "avivametalz.net/login" → PHISHING!

Multi-Channel Verification:

  • If you receive an unexpected email requesting action, verify through a separate channel
  • Call the person using a phone number from company directory (NOT from the email)
  • Walk to their office if they're in the building
  • Send a new email (don't reply to suspicious one) or use Teams/Slack
  • For customers: Use contact information from your CRM, not the email

Email Header Analysis:

  • Check the "Reply-To" address - does it match the sender?
  • Look at the full email headers for suspicious routing
  • Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication passes

💼 Business Email Compromise (BEC) Prevention

BEC attacks are highly targeted and can result in massive financial losses. Attackers research your company and impersonate executives or customers.

⚠️ Common BEC Scenarios:
• CEO/CFO requesting urgent wire transfers
• Customer changing banking information via email
• Vendor requesting payment to a new account
• Attorney requesting urgent payments for legal matters
• HR requesting W-2 information for "payroll audit"

AvivaMetals BEC Protection Protocol:

  • ALL wire transfer requests must be verified by phone using known numbers
  • Banking information changes require written confirmation via registered mail
  • Executive requests for financial transactions follow approval workflow
  • Never process urgent payments that bypass normal procedures
  • Be suspicious of requests for secrecy or confidentiality regarding payments

✅ What to Do When You Suspect Phishing

Immediate Actions:

  1. DON'T CLICK any links or download attachments
  2. DON'T REPLY to the email (confirms your address is active)
  3. DON'T DELETE it yet (IT Security may need to analyze it)
  4. DO REPORT it immediately to security@avivametals.com
  5. DO VERIFY through another channel if it seems to be from a colleague
  6. DO WARN others if it appears to target multiple employees

If You Already Clicked:

  • Don't panic - immediate action can prevent damage
  • Disconnect from network if you downloaded something
  • Change passwords immediately if you entered credentials
  • Report to IT Security immediately - the faster we know, the better
  • Contact your bank if you provided financial information
🏆 Remember: Reporting a phishing attempt is GOOD!
You're protecting the entire company. We never penalize employees for reporting suspicious emails, even false alarms. It's always better to report and be wrong than to ignore a real threat.

🛡️ Building Strong Security Habits

  • Use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA): Even if attackers get your password, MFA stops them
  • Use Strong, Unique Passwords: Different password for every account, use a password manager
  • Keep Software Updated: Security patches fix vulnerabilities that attackers exploit
  • Think Before You Click: Take 10 seconds to evaluate every unexpected email
  • Trust Your Instincts: If something feels off, it probably is
  • Stay Informed: Attend security training and read company security updates

📱 Mobile Device Security

Phishing isn't just on computers - mobile attacks are increasing:

  • SMS Phishing (Smishing): Fake texts claiming to be from banks, delivery services, or IT
  • Voice Phishing (Vishing): Phone calls from "tech support" or "fraud department"
  • App-Based Attacks: Fake apps that look like legitimate company tools
  • QR Code Scams: Malicious QR codes that lead to phishing sites
Mobile Safety Tips:
• Don't click links in unexpected text messages
• Verify caller identity before providing information
• Only download apps from official app stores
• Be cautious scanning QR codes from unknown sources
